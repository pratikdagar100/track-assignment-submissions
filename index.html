How to Track Assignment Submissions Without a Spreadsheet

Tracking submissions is the job nobody planned for. It appears when the work starts arriving in more than one place and at more than one time, and it usually ends up as a spreadsheet that is updated for three weeks and then abandoned. The alternative is not a better spreadsheet. It is collecting work in a way that tracks itself, and then doing three small checks at fixed moments.

About 4 minutes to read · Three checks per assignment

Why the spreadsheet stops being updated

Because it is a second system. The work arrives in one place and the record lives in another, so every submission needs two actions: receive it, then record it. Miss the second action a few times and the record is wrong; once it is wrong, nobody trusts it, and once nobody trusts it, nobody updates it.

The way out is to make the place the work arrives be the record. If every submission lands in a list with a name and a time, then “has this student submitted” is answered by looking, and nothing has to be transcribed.

Step 1: Collect each assignment into its own list

One list per task, with name, roll number, class, and the time of submission on every entry. This is what a submission link produces automatically. If you collect on paper, the nearest equivalent is a class list on the front of the pile, ticked as work is added, which is more work but the same idea.

The list must be per task. A single list for the term is the spreadsheet again.

Step 2: Keep received and reviewed as separate states

“Submitted” and “marked” are different facts and get confused constantly. A student who submitted on time and has not been marked yet is not late. A student who was marked from paper and never submitted online is not missing. Track them separately: received (with a time), late (received after the deadline), reviewed (marked, with feedback). Most confusion between a teacher and a student about homework is one of these being mistaken for another.

Step 3: Check the list against the register at two moments

The list tells you who submitted. Only the register tells you who did not, so compare them twice.

  1. 1
    The day before the deadline: names on the register that are not on the list get one message, by name, with the link. This is the reminder that works, because it is personal and early.
  2. 2
    The day after the deadline: names still missing are the follow-up list. Names on the list twice are one student resubmitting; keep the later one unless the page said otherwise.

Both checks take two minutes with the list and the register side by side, and they replace every other form of chasing.

Step 4: Handle late, incomplete and duplicate work by a rule, not a mood

Decide the rules once and write them where students see them. Late work: accepted and marked as late, or not accepted after a date. Incomplete work: marked as submitted for what is there, or returned for completion. Duplicates: the last one counts. When a case comes up, apply the rule. The point is not strictness; it is that the record stays meaningful, and students know what will happen.

Step 5: Use the record for the conversations it exists for

A per-task list with times and states answers the questions that used to need a search: has this student been submitting, how late is the class generally, which task did most people miss. When a parent or a colleague asks, the answer is a fact from the list, given in a sentence. Over a term, the lists show which students need a word before it becomes a pattern.

Worked example

Tracking one assignment for a class of 29

Wednesday, deadline Thursday 6pm: 21 on the list. Eight names from the register messaged individually: “The page 61 homework is due tomorrow 6pm; here is the link.”

Friday: 27 received, 3 of them late. Two missing, followed up in the lesson. One student appeared twice; the second submission was kept.

Reviewed: marked over the weekend from the list; each entry moved to reviewed with feedback.

The following week: a parent asked about their child’s homework. Answer, from the list: “Submitted Thursday at 5:40, marked Saturday, feedback on the page.”

Before you share it

  • Every assignment has its own list with name, roll number and submission time.
  • Received, late and reviewed are tracked as separate states.
  • The list is checked against the register the day before and the day after the deadline.
  • Rules for late, incomplete and duplicate work are written where students see them.
  • Reminders and follow-ups are sent by name, from the list.
